Built by operators

The story

Figtree's founders didn't set out to start a software company. They spent more than ten years running build-to-rent buildings, and pricing every home in a spreadsheet.

That is still how most of the industry does it. Rents are set from spreadsheets and gut feel, decisions get argued over in meetings, and money is left on the table when demand moves. Figtree recommends a rent for every home, using machine learning inside rules the operator sets.

Figtree sells to asset managers and investors, and most of them meet it first through its website. So we built the brand from scratch with the site in mind, then built the site: credible enough for an investment committee, warm enough to feel like it's about homes.

A site that moves like the product

Website

Three pages: Home, How it works and Built by Operators. As you scroll, the product rises out of the orange, the benefits stack like cards and a glowing light walks you through how the pricing works.

We built it on Squarespace with custom code for the motion, so Figtree's team can change the words themselves without calling us.

Illustration

Real dashboards are dense and change all the time, so we drew the product instead: simplified cards, charts and maps in the brand colours, each animated to show what a feature does. They explain the platform faster than a screenshot and don't go out of date.

The brand behind it

Logo

The wordmark is built from Gilroy Bold, heavy and plain, with one change: the dot of the i is a leaf. It's the only flourish in the logo, so it carries the whole idea of steady growth.

On its own the leaf becomes the icon, cut out of a rounded square. It's simple enough to read as a favicon and bold enough to sign off a slide.

Four colourways cover almost every use. The leaf stays orange wherever the background allows it.

Colour

Property technology defaults to blue. We went the other way, with an orange that feels like warm light rather than a warning sign. Peach gives it room to breathe, amber picks out highlights and a near-black charcoal carries the text.

It took a few rounds to get there. We tried electric blues, then golds and greens, then a family of sub-brands in orange, cyan and purple. Each round we cut back, until one colour family was left.

Early rounds: blue gradients, gold and green, and a sub-brand system for Rent, Build, Tech and Home. The leaf survived every one.

Typography

With the colour doing the shouting, the type stays calm. Circular Std Light sets every headline and paragraph, large and with plenty of space around it. Gilroy Bold appears only in the wordmark, so the logo never competes with the words around it.

Art direction

We didn't want photos of buildings. Instead the brand uses soft orange gradients with fine contour lines drawn over them, part map of rising ground, part rising chart. On screen they move slowly, like light shifting across a wall.

The glowing icon sits on top as the brand's signature image. In the product it turns into a spark wherever Figtree's assistant is working.

The icon becomes a spark for the digital assistant.

Stationery

Business cards, a letterhead and a laptop sticker. The sticker carries the team's six principles on the back, from "Every detail matters" to "Build with people who understand the problem", so the brand's values travel with the people who wrote them.
